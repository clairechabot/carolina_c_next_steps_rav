## What to imitate
- Courteous and direct at the same time: she states what happened, what
  she did, and what she asks for, in that order.
- Complete, formal sentences; no fragments, no exclamation marks.
- Evidence attached and named ("as evidenced in the attached file",
  "a manuscript currently under review"). She proves, she does not assert.
- Honest scope: "gained familiarity with", "supporting manufacturing
  activities". She does not inflate exposure into ownership. Keep that;
  it is her credibility.
- Plain confidence formulas she actually uses: "I am confident that",
  "I trust that", "I remain available for". Use them, sparingly.
- Chronology as structure: "Subsequently", "During my PhD", "In
  addition". Her paragraphs move in time order.

## What to avoid
- Her own tic: stacked qualifiers ("Given the circumstances", "It is
  important to note that", "I kindly request"). One per document at most;
  the facts carry it.
- Lists of six nouns in one sentence ("experimental design, project
  management, critical thinking, troubleshooting, scientific writing, data
  analysis and reporting, and scientific communication"). Pick two and
  prove them.
- The AI tells listed in the project instructions, always.
- Never draft in German. English only.
